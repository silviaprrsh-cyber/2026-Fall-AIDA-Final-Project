## 4. Time semantics and precipitation ETL

- Keep observation time, provider processing time, publication or actual transmission time when exposed, and local request/receipt time separate. If actual station transmission time is not explicitly available, leave it missing with an explanation; do not substitute observation, HTTP or local receipt timestamps.
- Preserve source precision, time zone and record identifiers; normalize internally to UTC and use America/Edmonton with daylight-saving handling for local display. Do not invent precise times for historical daily values. The latest observation and latest processed record may differ; retain their record associations.
- Build calculation windows from each water station's observation timestamps, independently of fetch times. For hourly hydrometry and 15-minute precipitation, sum nonoverlapping interval depths covering the corresponding hour. Integrate interval intensities over their durations when those are the source measurements. With complete valid coverage, retain total depth in mm and calculate mean intensity in mm/h by dividing total depth by the full window duration in hours; retain coverage as well.
- Example: four complete 15-minute depths of 1, 2, 0 and 1 mm yield 4 mm hourly depth and 4 mm/h mean intensity. Their arithmetic mean of 1 mm is not the hourly precipitation. Do not double-count overlapping 1-, 6- or 24-hour totals.
- Missing precipitation is not zero rainfall. Retain quality flags, interval boundaries, duration and units. If valid coverage is incomplete, set whole-window total depth and mean intensity to `null`; retain partial depth, covered duration, coverage and the gap reason separately. Do not present a partial-period mean as the whole-window mean, assume uniform rainfall across missing periods or prorate accumulations crossing window boundaries. Deduplicate revisions and late records by source, station and record identifier, supporting recalculation. Historical validation must not use information unavailable at the simulated time.
- Distinguish no returned records, historical-only data, stale observations, request failures and confirmed equipment faults. An empty query does not prove a station is offline; attach query scope and snapshot dates to status counts.

## 5. Spatial links and hydraulic constraints

- Link hydrometric IDs, weather IDs and section IDs through explicit association tables recording coordinates, CRS, provenance, distance, river and matching evidence. Nearby weather measurements are not observations at the hydrometric site. Proximity alone proves neither river connectivity nor valid water-level transfer.
- Prefer same-river topology, flow direction, along-river distance and confluences. Straight-line distance may screen candidates, but unverified relationships remain provisional. Having manual survey packages does not establish complete basin-wide profiles, bank elevations or datums.
- Section geometry gives wetted area A(H) at a specified level; geometry alone does not uniquely determine water level from discharge. Converting Q to H requires an applicable calibrated relationship or justified hydraulic method. V = Q/A requires quantities representing the same location and period.
- Verify gauge zero, vertical datums and relevant spill locations before comparing water surfaces with bank elevations. Do not mix CGVD28, CGVD2013, local datums or gauge-relative levels without validated conversion; stage is not automatically depth. Missing critical inputs require an indeterminate result and reason, not a low-risk default.
- L/V is only a preliminary water-transit estimate, not automatically flood-peak travel time. Validate effective lags against upstream and downstream hydrographs. Rainfall in mm cannot be added directly to discharge in m³/s; runoff conversion needs justification and must avoid contributions already captured by gauges.

## 6. Model and CV validation

- Evaluate estimates against interpretable baselines and independent observations, reporting errors, coverage, assumptions and uncertainty. Passing software tests does not validate hydraulics. Do not invent accuracy, risk thresholds or model completion.
- Sentinel-2 imagery is intermittent. Do not interpret clouds, shadows, ice or invalid pixels as dry land. Provider SCL/dataMask products are not this project's trained segmentation model. Separate training and validation by event, time and space to avoid neighboring-tile or same-event leakage.
- Compare water extent and width at actual image acquisition times and locations; planimetric water area cannot replace vertical cross-section area. Validate CV feedback and separate calibration from independent testing. Satellite imagery alone cannot validate hourly flood-peak timing.
