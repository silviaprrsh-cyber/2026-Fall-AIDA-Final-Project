These maps document how the **study extent** changed, not how flood hazard changed. The two historical maps are presentation redraws using the current A4 landscape “Overhead view” style. They preserve the original working geometry and the evidence available at that stage. They do not replace the current working map or validate a forecast.

## Why the scope was reduced

The first reduction was recorded on **October 12, 2026** in the revised scope proposal: reliable observations did not cover enough tributary inflows across the full basin. Filling widespread gaps with estimates would compound uncertainty. Dickson to Joffre retained a better-observed mainstem and the relevant tributary input-to-mouth segments; this did not make the reach fully observed. The **November 4, 2026** reduction stopped at 05CC002 because the selected observations provide no comparable downstream constraint beyond that gauge. It removed GIS points P06–P11 and downstream Blindman/Waskasoo inputs rather than implying the model was validated that far. See the [current scope audit](../data/reports/scope_05CC002.json). The October 4 Week 6 feedback supported a first version using a small set of reaches and gauges, historical and held-out checks, and separate treatment of live operations. It did not identify Joffre or 05CC002 as required endpoints. The two reductions are project scope decisions, not a controlled sensitivity test or evidence that the instructor approved a revised proposal.

## Source and interpretation

- The original basemap/source catalogue is retained in the sibling local archive `Final_project/data/processed/qgis_layers_20261011/red_deer_sources.qgz`; its associated manifest and vector inputs distinguish ECCC hydrometry/precipitation, Alberta survey lines, NRCan river geometry and schematic weather areas. The first reduction is reproducible from the archived QGIS project and its relative GeoJSON layers in [the pre-05CC002 rollback archive](../data/processed/scope_history/before_05CC002_20261104.zip). The [older Joffre export](../data/processed/scope_history/Overhead_view_Joffre.pdf) is retained separately. The current QGIS file [`dickson_to_joffre.qgz`](../project/dickson_to_joffre.qgz) retains its legacy filename, but its extent is now 05CC002.
- The Overhead style makes comparison easy: title, nominated reach, legend, north arrow, and source attribution. The maps use different scales; compare retained features and scope, not page lengths. ECCC supplies station locations and archival records; NRCan NHN supplies river geometry; government survey sources supply cross-section lines; OpenStreetMap provides reference context. Both redraws used preserved local OSM tiles with attribution, with no new tile download. The full-project map displays the original source layers and does not add a calculated mainstem route or new target layer. A line near another feature is not proof of flow direction, a network connection or valid hydraulic transfer.
- Blue/yellow in the reduced maps refer to the **dated archive review**: blue denotes a qualifying recent observation at that review; yellow denotes a historical or lake reference and does not prove that a station is offline. The full-project redraw uses yellow to identify historical reference records; its catalogue counts are not the same blue/yellow recent-availability test. Green weather associations are schematic, not catchments or verified rainfall coverage. A cross-section is not a water-level reading.
- The red GIS points were manually selected to examine bends and nearby locations of potential overbank concern. Mapped sections, bridges and confluences locate them reproducibly; they are not gauges, default calibration targets, validated locations, or calculated flood risk. In the historical Joffre map, GIS **P06** is downstream between the Gaetz Avenue and 49 Avenue bridges. In the V4 receiving app, **P06** is the model's terminal output at **05CC002**; it is not the old GIS P06. The 05CC002 official station coordinate was not moved; the current selected endpoint projected onto the mapped centerline is **69.4 m** away.
- Horizontal placement and vertical datum are different. Geographic coordinates are displayed in QGIS with the OSM-compatible projection; measured route distances use a local metric CRS, not PDF page scale. Survey elevations retain their reported vertical references; some historical stations have unconfirmed source coordinates/datum. A map overlay does not establish a shared gauge zero, comparable depths or banks, or model accuracy. The project’s November 10 review recorded a request for gauge-zero elevation, vertical datum and effective dates through the official WSC data/service form. A later WSC response supplied ten-year Benchmark History Reports and directed the project to the Water Office datum remarks and effective ranges; it warned that datum changes can interrupt comparability. The response is source evidence, not proof that every gauge and survey section share a usable datum. The [AIDA 2156 data-source notes](../../AIDA-2156/docs/DATA_SOURCES.md) document the later terminal depth-proxy reference separately; they do not validate other points.
